Relief shading

Mountains and ice domes show in light and shadow, but the globe stays a smooth sphere. Each pair is the same frame with the relief map on and off.

What to look for

The ice dome's east-facing half catches the morning Sun and is a little brighter, and the brightness changes smoothly. Gentle ice slopes are hard to store in an 8-bit image without visible contour bands; the relief map is encoded to avoid them, and this is the shot that would show them.

The Arctic Ocean is open water in every shot: NASA's Blue Marble map has no sea ice.

Clouds at eight distances

NASA's cloud map sits on its own see-through sphere 10 km above the ground and is lit exactly like the ground. Look for flicker between clouds and ground, and for orbit lines cutting through the clouds.

Known limits

Up close the clouds are soft: NASA serves this map at 2048 px, about 20 km per pixel.

The clouds are a 2001 composite, so they do not move or match the date on screen.

Review, 2026-11-19

An independent reviewer read the whole branch at d6ce25c, decoded the shipped files and checked them against the source data: no critical problems, map orientation and licence records confirmed. Its three must-fix findings are fixed.

Fixed

Orbit lines cut slits through the clouds

The clouds now draw before other see-through objects (777f497).

Fixed

Speckled maps on drivers that work pixel by pixel

A safety margin in the map seam helper, with a GPU test that failed on a third of the pixels before the fix (a61601a). Moon and Mars shared the bug.

Fixed

The browser test checked too early for cloud shader errors

The app marks the page when every map is applied and the test waits for it (ae40e1a).

7 smaller notes, logged for later
  • If the colour map fails, the other three maps stay in video memory unused.
  • Two loader tests could also check that the other layers stay off.
  • One map lookup uses a slightly unnormalized position, up to 0.6 pixel off near 80°.
  • Thick cloud at the edge of the globe hides the blue atmosphere glow.
  • Rebuilding the maps byte for byte needs the same library versions, which should be written down.
  • Large lakes are levelled to sea level, not lake level, for the relief map; the measured effect is small.
  • A guard against building the cloud layer after Earth is disposed.
