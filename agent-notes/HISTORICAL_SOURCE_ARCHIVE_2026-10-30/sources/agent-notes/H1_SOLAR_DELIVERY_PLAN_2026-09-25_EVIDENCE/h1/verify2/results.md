
# ux-a11y-layout ship-with-fixes
I built branch fix/solar-data-status-notice at 7c791dc and main at bd48a7d, served them on 4192 and 4193, and measured with Playwright (swiftshader, DPR 1, requests to :8042 aborted in-browser so the other verifier's server was never contacted).

Accessibility passes. axe-core 4.10.2 found 0 violations on both builds in all five chip states at 1440x900, so nothing is new against main. The two 'incomplete' items are expected: color-contrast is undeterminable on every chip because the chips sit over the canvas, and the aria-label on #status-bar was already flagged on main. Chip text contrast is 15.5:1 over space and 10.9:1 over the Sun disc. #status-solar is a plain generic node with no name, no live region and no description. #status-solar-announcer is role=status (polite, atomic) and 1x1 clipped. It is not focusable: it was never reached in 80 Tab presses. It was empty at DOMContentLoaded and first written at 3582 ms, in the same tick as the chip's error state. Writing it changed no scroll size or bar box and produced no layout-shift entry.

Layout has one real regression. At widths from 701 to 1100 px, in every Sun state, the new chip adds a row, so the chips cover the 'Drag orbit / Scroll zoom / Double-click' hint. At 1024x768, 41.7% of the hint text is hidden; at 768x1024 it is 100%. Main hides none of it. A 3-line CSS change moves the hint to the top at ≤1100 px, and it was verified in the page: 0% hidden and no collision with the toolbar.

The status bar is taller than on main at every viewport:
- 1440x900: +0 to +29 px. With the panel open it is always 2 or 3 rows, and the Asteroids chip is squeezed onto 2 lines.
- 1024x768: +37 px.
- 768x1024: +37 to +50 px.
- 390x844: +45 to +59 px. On main the bar is 61 px; on the branch it is 106 to 120 px.

No text was clipped and nothing overflowed horizontally at any viewport or state. The URL was never broken mid-token.

Legibility is confirmed as a problem. At 1x, Chakra Petch renders 127.0.0.1 as '1270.01', both at 10.88 px and at 10.24 px, and 8042 shows a gap ('80 42') at 10.88 px. No CSS-only fix works. letter-spacing 0.04em (and 0.08em) still merges at least one dot. tabular-nums does nothing: the URL width stays 105.7 px. Weight 600 still merges dots at 390 px. Putting the URL in its own span fixes it at both sizes. The system-ui option is the smallest change (+16% URL width, platform font dependent). A monospace stack at 0.95em (+29%) separates the dots by construction. The span must sit inside one inner wrapper, because the chip is inline-flex.

Copy is understandable and consistent with 'Asteroids: procedural fallback (...)'. Four changes are clearly better:
- The timeout text: under the default setup (8-10 s per request) it is the permanent message and reads as 'server down'.
- The CORS text: remove the nested parentheses and put the remedy in console.warn.
- The server-error text: shorten it.
- Add hysteresis for backend-to-stale changes. A simulated run that alternated success and failure produced 12 announcements in 57 s.

Title tooltips never show, because the bar has pointer-events:none and the element at the chip's centre is #space-canvas.

Housekeeping: on arrival, orphaned vite previews from the earlier interrupted ux attempt held ports 4192 and 4193 (PIDs 1717676-1717679, recorded in h1/verify/ux/pids.txt). I stopped them by PID. I also stopped my own previews (PIDs 2246293-2246297) by PID, and ss confirms 4192 and 4193 are free. Both :8042 listeners belong to other verifiers and were left alone. The wt-notice and wt-h1 worktree status is unchanged.

# browser-matrix (fix/solar-data-status-notice @ 7c791dc, real app via vite dev 127.0.0.1:5191 / preview 127.0.0.1:4191, headless Chromium + SwiftShader) ship-with-fixes
I ran all 7 requested scenarios plus two extra ones (S8, the server starting mid-session; S9, the server dropping and coming back). The classifier, the chip text, the state attribute, the one-announcement-per-situation rule and the console.warn pairing all behave as specified in every scenario. S4 is marked fail because a false startup error preceded the "connected" announcement.

What passed:
- Blocked origin: CORS wording for :5191 (S2, 0 flips in 170 s) and for the preview origin :4191 (S7).
- Unreachable (S1): 2 health probes, then the verdict is reused for about 33 s.
- Slow or busy (S3): timeout and busy alternate 10 times on the chip but produce exactly 1 announcement.
- Mock errors: 429, 503 "timed out", CORS-less 500 and non-JSON or [] bodies each get the right wording and 1 announcement. An AIA tile 404 gives "AIA 304 image failed to load".
- Stale after the mock is killed (S4), and back to backend after it returns (S9, re-announced).
- Real data with the timeout raised (S6): the chip shows "hybrid forcing, observations blended 41%; AIA 304 image". That matches the captured body (mode hybrid, applied true, blend_weight 0.4058, preferred_channel aia_304, image 192425468, tile HTTP 200).
- The chip has no role or aria-live. The announcer is role=status and visually hidden. Chip title always equals its text. No page errors anywhere.

Defect found (major): with a fast backend, 4 of 6 cold loads first show a false "did not answer within 2.6 s" error. The page announces "Sun data unavailable: solar server too slow or busy" and warns in the console. About 0.2–4 s later a retry switches it to "connected". Cause: the 2.6 s abort timer is still running while the reply body is read. A main-thread stall at startup (2–3 s, mostly shader compile under SwiftShader) lets the timer fire before the body finishes, and the abort is reported as a timeout. The same race once turned the first 503 "timed out" into "busy".

## findings
- [major] With a fast backend, a main-thread stall during startup is reported as a 2.6 s server timeout, causing a false error chip, a false 'too slow or busy' announcement and a console.warn @ src/frontend/features/solar/solarPhysicsClient.ts:215-218 (the abort timer stays armed after fetch resolves) and :244-246 (`throw timedOut ? this.timeoutError() : ...` in the json() catch); the forced first request is issued at src/main.ts:1396 just before the heavy first frames; retry backoff at solarPhysicsClient.ts:277 / sunController.ts:561 keeps the false state for about 4–6 s
  EVIDENCE: Fact, headless SwiftShader. cd pw && OUT=verify2/browser RUNS=3 SEC=10 node h1w-browser-startup.mjs with the mock in ok mode. Run 1: '543ms physics call; 1219ms resolved 200 cors; 1219ms json() called; 3328ms signal-abort; 3328ms json() rejected AbortError; 3328ms announcer "Sun data unavailable: solar server too slow or busy..."; chip error "did not answer within 2.6 s"; 7275ms call #2 -> 7287ms "connected"'. The stall was '1219ms +2108ms'. Run 2 was the same (stall 1269ms +1954ms). Run 3 was clean. The same false timeout also appeared in S4 (request 200 in 4 ms at 0.99 s, chip error at 4.47 s) and S5f (error at 6.37 s): 4 of 6 cold loads against the fast mock. A CPU profile (startup-stall-profile.json) puts the startup cost mostly in '(program)' 1955 ms and three.js onFirstUse 536 ms (shader compile). Inference: on a real GPU the stall is likely shorter, so the rate there is unknown. The race exists whenever a stall of 2.6 s or more starts between headers and body. Today's 6–12 s backend always times out anyway, so the bug will show once H-1's root is fixed.
  FIX: Once `fetch` resolves (headers received), clear `timeoutHandle` and read the body without the timeout racing it. Pass a separate body-read budget, or give `response.json()` no shared abort signal. Or keep the timer but record `headersAt`: when the timer fires after headers arrived, or fires more than ~500 ms late (`performance.now() - scheduledAt > timeoutMs + 500`, which means a main-thread stall), retry immediately without setting lastFailure. Belt and braces: in describeSolarDataStatus/SolarDataAnnouncer, don't leave 'connecting' on a single 'timeout' (require consecutiveFailures >= 2 for the timeout kind). Add a unit test: headers resolve, json() stays pending, timers advance past timeoutMs, expect no 'timeout' failure.
- [minor] A cached 'unreachable' probe verdict turns a just-started, origin-refusing server into 'did not answer within 2.6 s' / 'too slow or busy' for up to 30 s @ src/frontend/features/solar/solarPhysicsClient.ts:328 (verdict reused for HEALTH_PROBE_REUSE_MS=30000, :55) combined with :306-307 (in the timeout branch any cached verdict other than 'origin-refused' becomes a timeout)
